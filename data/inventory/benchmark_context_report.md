# Benchmark Context Report: SUMMIT v4 Part A

> Generated by `scripts/analyze_benchmark_context.py`. Do not edit by hand.
> Re-run after any fixture change; do not hand-edit the numbers below.

## Summary

Top-1 structure retrieval was evaluated on **207 plant natural products** drawn from the SUMMIT v4 benchmark set. Each compound was ranked against a decoy pool of candidate structures whose size varied widely (median 48; interquartile range 16-122; range 5-642). Overall top-1 accuracy was **92.3%** (191/207 compounds), with all misassignments ranked second or third rather than retrieved as an unrelated scaffold. Accuracy was lowest on the smallest decoy pools (`<10`, 79.3%) and highest on mid-sized pools (`50-200`, 95.2%), so the headline figure is not propped up by easy small-pool cases. The site-localisation task covered 36 compounds at a median of 3 possible edit sites, with observed accuracy of 88.9% against a 44.3% chance baseline; however 15/36 (42%) of those compounds offered at most two possible sites, so that baseline is weak. These figures are a **reproduction of the SUMMIT v4 benchmark's own outputs, not an independent held-out validation.**

## 0. Reproduction, not independent validation

The per-compound results were produced by the SUMMIT v4 benchmark itself and copied verbatim from its output directory. The numbers below are therefore a REPRODUCTION of SUMMIT v4, not an independent validation.

**Consequence:** the 92.3% figure carries no information about how the method generalises to structures outside the SUMMIT v4 benchmark set. It is a consistency check on our reproduction, not evidence of out-of-sample performance. Any external document quoting this number must label it a reproduction (see ADR-0036, which supersedes the narrower ADR-0027).

### Reading this table

Accuracy is **not** simply a function of pool size. The weakest bucket is `<10` at 79.3% and the strongest is `50-200` at 95.2%. Notably the overall 92.3% is **not** inflated by easy small pools: the small pools are where the method does worst. The likely reason is that the decoy pool for a compound is built from structures similar to it, so a small pool is enriched in near-isomers while a large pool adds progressively more dissimilar structures. That mechanism is a hypothesis about how the benchmark set was constructed, not something these fixtures can prove, since they record only pool sizes and ranks, not decoy identities.

**What this table supports.** The headline is not carried by the easiest pool size: the `<10` bucket holds only 29 compounds and scores *below* the overall figure. **What it does not support.** A uniform-capability reading. The spread between weakest and strongest bucket is 15.9 percentage points, and because the weakest bucket is small a handful of compounds shift it by several points. Quote the breakdown, not the single number.

### Structural pattern (RDKit)

Across the 16 failures with parsable structures:

- **16/16** share the molecular formula of the correct answer, i.e. they are constitutional isomers, not unrelated structures.
- **0/16** are pure stereoisomers (identical once stereochemistry is stripped).
- **15/16** have the same ring count.
- **7/16** contain a pyranose sugar ring.
- Molecular weight of the true structure: median **640 Da**, mean 731 Da; **4/16** are above 800 Da.
- Mean Morgan-fingerprint Tanimoto similarity to the truth: **0.81**.

The dominant failure mode is therefore **regio-/constitutional isomer discrimination**, not retrieval of the wrong scaffold. Every miss has the correct molecular formula, and all but one keep the correct ring count, so the method lands on the right scaffold family and then picks the wrong regioisomer. This is the regime where 1H-13C HSQC alone is least informative, because moving a substituent or a sugar linkage shifts only a few carbons.

**The two structural patterns are distinct.** 7/16 failures are glycosides, and those are also the heavy ones (504-1545 Da). The remaining 9 are non-glycosidic, highly oxygenated scaffolds (338-716 Da). So there is no single "high molecular weight" story: glycosylation drives one group of failures and dense oxygenation drives the other, and they fail for the same underlying reason -- attachment or linkage position changes too few HSQC cross-peaks to separate them.

**What the failures are not.** None of the 16 is a pure stereoisomer of the correct answer, so stereochemical discrimination is not the failure mode in this benchmark. That is worth stating explicitly because stereoisomer pairs are often assumed to be the hard case; here every miss is a constitutional isomer, which is why Tanimoto similarity to the truth stays high (0.81).

## 4. Site-localisation benchmark audit

The localisation benchmark covers **36 compounds** with a **median of 3 possible edit sites** (IQR 2.0-6.5, range 1-29).

Reported accuracy is 88.9% against a chance baseline of 44.3%.

> **WARNING -- the baseline is weak.** **15 of 36 compounds (42%) have at most two possible sites** (8 have exactly one, where the answer is forced). On a two-site problem even random guessing scores 50%. The 88.9% headline must therefore **not** be presented as a strong localisation capability. It is a mixture of vacuous single-site cases, near-coin-flip two-site cases, and a genuinely hard remainder that scores 81.0%.

### Accuracy split by problem difficulty

| Subset | Compounds | Share | Top-1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 possible site (vacuous) | 8 | 22% | 100.0% |
| 2 possible sites (50% by guessing) | 7 | 19% | 100.0% |
| 3-6 possible sites | 12 | 33% | 83.3% |
| 7+ possible sites | 9 | 25% | 77.8% |
| *Subtotal: >2 possible sites (genuinely hard)* | *21* | *58%* | **81.0%** |
| All | 36 | 100% | 88.9% |

The subtotal row aggregates the `3-6` and `7+` buckets, so the per-bucket rows sum to 36 while the subtotal is a subset, not an extra group.

### Why the published chance baseline is too generous

The published 44.3% chance figure is the **mean of 1/n_sites**, which single-site cases pull up to 100% by definition. On the 28 compounds with two or more sites the observed accuracy is **85.7%**, against a baseline nearer 1/median(n) = 33.3%. Neither figure is wrong; they simply measure different populations, and the pairing of 88.9% with 44.3% flatters the method because both are computed over all 36 compounds.

**Bottom line.** The defensible claim is that on compounds with more than two candidate sites (21 of 36), top-1 localisation reached 81.0%. The 88.9% figure should never be quoted on its own: it is inflated by 15 near-trivial cases. An 88.9% result on a median 3-site problem is a much weaker signal than the same number on a 10-site problem.

## 5. How to cite these numbers

- Quote **92.3% top-1** only as *reproduction of SUMMIT v4* (ADR-0036).
- Always pair it with **median candidate pool 48** and the bucket breakdown.
- Do not quote **88.9% localisation** on its own. Quote **81.0% on the 21 compounds with more than two possible sites**, and state that 15/36 cases had at most two sites.
- Do not describe either number as independent validation (ADR-0036).
